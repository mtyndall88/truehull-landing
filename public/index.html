How it works

Three steps, one document you can print.

Paste the listing

Drop in listing text and TrueHull pulls out year, make, model, length, type, state, HIN, price and engine setup. Or type the details yourself.

TrueHull reviews and flags

Risk rules read the details you gave (engine hours, seller-note language, HIN format, and local storm history) and mark what deserves a question.

Work the checklist

You get the official records to pull for your state, the questions to ask the seller, and the spots to inspect. Print it and take it to the boat.

Where the guidance comes from

Real hurricane tracks, not a vague warning.

TrueHull compares the vessel location's representative point - your ZIP code where we have one, otherwise the county - against NOAA/NHC storm-track data and lists the named storms in the Atlantic, Gulf and eastern Pacific basins that passed nearby, with dates and approximate distances you can ask about.

What this does not tell you

This identifies possible regional exposure near the submitted location. It does not verify where the vessel was during a storm or whether it was damaged, and it is not an insurance, title, salvage, registration, USCG or law-enforcement record check. Matching runs in all 50 states and DC for the Atlantic, Gulf and eastern Pacific basins, at ZIP precision where a ZIP is provided.

The part buyers get stuck on

Boat records are scattered across state and federal agencies. We route you.

Every report routes you to the official checks for your boat: state title, registration and lien records, the federal theft and documentation files, and a state stolen-boat search where the state offers one - each with the agency link and the identifiers it needs. We route you in all 50 states, to official and authoritative sources.

About

Why TrueHull exists

Buying a used boat means trusting a stranger's description of something most people cannot easily inspect. The paperwork is fragmented: titles and registration sit with individual state agencies, theft records sit with law enforcement, and storm history sits in federal weather data. Each one lives somewhere different, and none of it is obvious to a first-time buyer.

TrueHull was built to organize that process. It takes the listing details you already have and turns them into a checklist: what the details flag, which agency handles your state, what to ask the seller, and what to look at when you stand in front of the boat.

We are deliberate about what the product will not claim. The sections that make a data claim state their own limits, and the report tells you plainly what it cannot verify. A tool that overstates itself is worse than no tool at all when the purchase is this large.

Where it stands today

TrueHull is early-stage and in active development. The report engine, ZIP- and county-level storm matching in all 50 states for the Atlantic, Gulf and eastern Pacific basins, 50-state routing to official state and federal checks, and HIN format review all work today. The public buyer experience is being finished now.

Who it is for

First-time buyers who want a guided process, remote buyers evaluating boats in another state, and organized researchers who want one place to keep it all. Brokers, dealers and surveyors are welcome too: TrueHull routes buyers toward professional inspection, never away from it.

What TrueHull does not verify

TrueHull does not access or verify official records. A report does not confirm ownership, title status, liens, theft or salvage history, insurance claims, accident history, registration, or USCG documentation status, and it does not verify where a vessel was during a storm or whether it was damaged. Nothing here replaces a marine survey, a sea trial, or a title and lien search. Every report recommends them.
